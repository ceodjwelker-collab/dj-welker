A one-off deliverable is a logo, a website, a nice PDF. You pay, you get the file, and a month later you're back where you started because nothing around it changed. I stopped selling those. What I actually build is the machine that keeps making the thing — brand, website, workflows, software, and the customer experience wired together as one connected operation. Build it, connect it, automate the repetitive parts, then run it. You own the result.

That's not a slogan I picked up. It's how I work because I came up in operations — assistant manager shifts, customer service, running the floor. I know what it feels like when the tools don't talk to each other and leads fall through the cracks between them. So when I design something, I'm not thinking about how it looks in a portfolio. I'm thinking about the Tuesday morning six months from now when someone has to actually run it.

Which brings me to the button in the nav that says Proof Mode.

Portfolios lie. Not always on purpose — but "concept" quietly becomes "case study," a personal side project gets photographed like a paying client, and a number nobody measured ends up in a headline. I didn't want to build another one of those. So I built the opposite into the site itself. Hit Proof Mode and every project shows its real status: launched client work, or self-built concept, or shelved. Role, constraints, honest status. No pretending.

Here's the line I don't cross: never invent titles, degrees, or clients. Never claim a result I can't show you. When Titanium Detailing is the only paying client so far, I say so — one verified client, live, 5.0 stars. When something like Shiftly or STAKE is my own venture with real working UI but no customers yet, it's labeled concept, and there are no fake logos or made-up revenue attached to it. Everything true, framed in the best honest light.

Building the proof directly into the product is the most on-brand thing I could do, because the whole pitch is *systems you can trust to run without you.* A portfolio that polices its own honesty is just that idea pointed at myself. If I'll be straight with you about my own work before you've paid me a dollar, that tells you how I'll build yours.
